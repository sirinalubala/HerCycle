Your Body, Your Health 💗

Menstruation is a natural biological process. Every person's experience is different, and understanding the menstrual cycle can help people take better care of their health.

1. The Basics 🌸

What is Menstruation?

Menstruation, commonly called a period, is the natural process in which the uterus sheds its lining through the vagina when pregnancy does not occur.

Menstrual Phase

The uterine lining sheds, resulting in menstrual bleeding.

Follicular Phase

Hormones support the growth of ovarian follicles and the uterine lining.

Ovulation

An ovary releases an egg. The timing can vary from cycle to cycle.

Luteal Phase

Hormones prepare the uterine lining. If pregnancy does not occur, hormone levels fall and another period begins.

Common Period Symptoms

Cramps, headaches, bloating, fatigue, acne, mood changes and food cravings are some symptoms people may experience.

2. Be There 💙

Supporting someone during menstruation does not require anything extraordinary. Small acts of understanding, kindness and respect can make a difference.

At Home 🏠

Support your mother, sister or other family members. Help them when they need something and respect their privacy.

With Friends and Classmates 🫂

Never make fun of someone experiencing period symptoms. Offer help if they need a sanitary product or someone to talk to.

In Relationships 💗

Listen, be patient and understand that everyone experiences menstruation differently. Never dismiss someone's discomfort.

In Public Places 🤝

If someone experiences an unexpected period, help them find a restroom or sanitary product without embarrassing them.

Things You Can Do

  • Listen without judging.
  • Help someone get a sanitary product if needed.
  • Respect their personal space and privacy.
  • Never make insensitive jokes about periods.
  • Encourage respectful conversations.

3. Myth vs Fact 💡

Let's replace common misconceptions with facts.

Myth: Periods are dirty.

Fact: Menstruation is a natural biological process. Menstrual blood is not dirty or impure.

Myth: Everyone has a 28-day cycle.

Fact: Cycle lengths vary from person to person and can change over time.

Myth: Period pain should always be ignored.

Fact: Severe or disruptive pain deserves medical attention.

Myth: Talking about periods is embarrassing.

Fact: Menstrual health is a normal health topic that should be discussed respectfully.

Myth: Boys don't need to learn about periods.

Fact: Everyone can benefit from understanding menstrual health.

Myth: Periods always stop people from exercising.

Fact: Many people can exercise during their periods if they feel comfortable.

4. Period Hygiene 🧼

  • Change sanitary pads every few hours or sooner when needed.
  • Follow product instructions for tampons and menstrual cups.
  • Wash hands before and after changing menstrual products.
  • Clean the external genital area gently with water.
  • Avoid scented products and internal vaginal washes.
  • Dispose of used products hygienically.

5. Nutrition and Self-Care 🥗

  • Include iron-rich foods such as beans, spinach and fortified foods.
  • Eat a balanced diet with fruits, vegetables and protein.
  • Drink enough water throughout the day.
  • Try gentle movement or walking if it feels comfortable.
  • Get adequate sleep and allow yourself to rest.
  • A warm compress may help ease menstrual cramps.

6. When Should You Consult a Doctor? 🩺

  • Periods are repeatedly very heavy or last longer than usual.
  • Menstrual pain is severe or affects everyday activities.
  • Your periods suddenly become very irregular.
  • You experience bleeding between periods.
  • You miss periods unexpectedly and pregnancy is possible.
  • You experience unusual discharge, fever or pelvic pain.

Seek urgent medical care for extremely heavy bleeding, fainting, severe sudden pain or other alarming symptoms.

10. Remember 💕

There is no single perfect menstrual cycle for everyone. Cycle length, symptoms and experiences can differ. Tracking patterns can help people understand changes, but it cannot replace medical advice.
