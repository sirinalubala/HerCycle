Period Emergency Guide 🩷

Know what to do when something doesn't feel right. Your health and safety always come first.

🚨 When to Get Immediate Medical Help

  • Bleeding that soaks through a pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours, especially with weakness or dizziness.
  • Fainting, severe dizziness, breathlessness or chest pain.
  • Sudden, severe pelvic or abdominal pain.
  • Severe pain or bleeding when pregnancy is possible.
  • High fever with severe pelvic pain or feeling very unwell.

Do not wait for symptoms to disappear. Seek urgent medical care or contact emergency services.

1. Unexpected Period in Public 🩸

What can you do?

  • Stay calm. Periods are natural.
  • Find a restroom and check the bleeding.
  • Use a sanitary pad or another suitable menstrual product.
  • If you do not have one, ask someone you trust for help.
  • If clothing is stained, use a jacket or other covering if available.

2. Severe Period Cramps 😣

What can help?

  • Try a warm compress or heating pad wrapped in cloth.
  • Rest and drink enough water.
  • Gentle walking or stretching may help if comfortable.
  • Ask a healthcare professional about suitable pain relief.
  • Seek medical advice if pain is severe, new or affects daily life.

3. Heavy Menstrual Bleeding 🩸

Watch for these signs:

  • Changing pads or tampons every 1–2 hours repeatedly.
  • Bleeding for more than 7 days.
  • Passing large blood clots.
  • Feeling unusually tired, weak or short of breath.

Contact a healthcare professional for evaluation. If bleeding is very heavy or you feel faint or breathless, seek urgent medical help.

4. Feeling Dizzy or Weak 💫

  • Sit or lie down somewhere safe.
  • Tell someone nearby how you are feeling.
  • Avoid standing suddenly.
  • Seek medical care if dizziness continues or is severe.
  • Fainting, chest pain or breathlessness needs urgent attention.

5. Period Product Problems 🧼

  • Follow the instructions for your menstrual product.
  • Change pads regularly and wash your hands before and after.
  • Do not leave tampons in longer than the product instructions allow (usually no more than 8 hours).
  • If you cannot remove a tampon or menstrual cup, seek medical help.
  • Sudden fever, vomiting, rash, dizziness or feeling very unwell while using a tampon requires urgent medical attention.

⚠️ Important Reminder

Severe pain, unusual bleeding and sudden changes should never be dismissed as something you must simply tolerate. This guide provides general information and does not replace professional medical advice.

📞 Emergency Help in India

112 – National Emergency Response Support System

If you or someone nearby is experiencing a medical emergency, contact emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department.

Remember: Asking for help is not weakness. Your health matters, and you deserve proper care and support. 💗
